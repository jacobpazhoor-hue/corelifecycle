EVALUATE against this rubric. For each problem, name the scene id or the frame file.
1. ACCURACY — do the dates, dollar figures, percentages, named people, quotations and the explained
   MECHANISM match docs/research/<slug>.md and real life? Flag anything wrong, exaggerated beyond
   the research, or unverified. A confidently-stated invented number is a HIGH-severity issue.
2. STORYLINE & RETENTION (the most important rubric — BIBLE.md §3–§7) —
   - THE HOOK, first ~30s, five steps IN ORDER: (1) shock stat or paradox in ONE sentence,
     (2) escalating consequence fragments, (3) the "But…" paradox turn, (4) a thesis naming the
     subject ("This is the story of…" or, for a systemic subject, a question thesis), (5) a hard cut
     to DATE + PLACE. A weak or slow first ~15s is HIGH severity. NO logo, NO "in this video", NO
     channel name, NO subscribe — anywhere in the narration.
   - VOICE: THIRD PERSON, PAST TENSE throughout. Flag any second-person framing; "you" is seasoning
     only (2.6–13.4 per 1000 words, inside a general truth), never the frame.
   - SENTENCE BANDS (BIBLE §3, measured): average 7.5–10.6 words, MEDIAN 6–9, and 40–59% of
     sentences UNDER 8 words. 29–68 explicit numbers across the episode (~2–3.5/min). Flag a script
     that drifts to a 15-word average — that is the retired canon's rhythm.
   - STRUCTURE: 3–5 chapters, chronological, each named in the two-part `Evocative Noun: Plain
     Explanation` form and drawn by a card=dict(kind="chapter") on its first scene; every chapter
     ends on a forward hook that is a question the next chapter answers.
   - MECHANISM: the thing being explained is NAMED, DEFINED in one short sentence, then SHOWN
     working. That is the whole promise of the format — flag an episode that narrates events without
     ever explaining the machine.
   - CAUSALITY: beats connect by but/therefore, never "and then." SPECIFICITY over abstraction.
   - ONE MIDPOINT REVERSAL (~50%) where the subject's own machine turns on them, with gap=1.4 on the
     scene immediately before it. ONE recurring factual motif re-triggered at each escalation. ONE
     share-worthy "wait — that's real?" fact.
   - DIALOGUE is a primary device: several short in-world EXCHANGES (2–4 lines) that REPLACE
     narration explaining the same beat.
   - PROMISE→PAYOFF: check the ledger comment atop content.py — every planted setup pays off.
   - THE ENDING: thematic reflection → a direct QUOTE or a callback to the opening image → often a
     forward tease; complicit first-person-plural is characteristic. NEVER plot resolution, NEVER a
     call to action.
   - Coherent, no contradictions, no repeated lines.
3. CRAYON STYLE + VISUAL DEFECTS (CRAYON_BIBLE §3 §5 §6, BIBLE §8) —
   - ART: clean FLAT VECTOR — uniform-weight black outline, flat fills, no texture, no grain, no
     gradients on characters. Each scene commits to a dominant colour key. The grey anonymous crowd
     with a single full-colour hero is the channel's focal device.
   - CAMERA IS LOCKED. No dolly, push-in, drift or parallax sway; motion is local to characters and
     props. A drifting frame is a HIGH-severity style regression.
   - TEMPLATES: only the thirteen explainer environments may appear — officeFloor, boardroom,
     exchangeFloor, cityStreet, domesticInterior, newsMontage, bankExterior, courtHearing,
     factoryFloor, broadcastDesk, crowdQueue, closeUpPortrait, chartBoard. Flag any frame drawn in
     the old line-art-on-warm-paper style. Never the same template on two ADJACENT scenes, and no
     room used much more than once per 10 scenes (an over-used room reads as "the same picture
     again"; the fix is to PLACE IT LESS and vary what surrounds it, never to add camera motion).
   - PERIOD: no era-marked machine under pre-1900 narration (CRT monitors, projectors, parked cars,
     desk phones, glass skylines). The fix is period="pre1900" on that SCENE in content.py.
   - DEVICE VARIETY: full-screen card= beats, panels= splits, foreground= over-the-shoulder
     silhouettes, bubbles=/floating dialogue, closeUpPortrait cut-ins should all appear. A run of
     plain full-frame rooms is a defect.
   - DEFECTS: detached or broken limbs; a face missing an eye/brow, or NO face where a character
     should have one; props broken/duplicated/misaligned/floating; elements off the edge or
     overlapping the text; two ADJACENT scenes that look identical; anything unfinished.
