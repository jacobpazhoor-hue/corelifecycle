
# Research: FBI Undercover Agent — "Your Life as an Undercover Agent at Every Level"

## The real ladder (8 rungs, elaborated for pacing)

1. **New Agent Trainee (NAT)** — Quantico, **21 weeks** of training (firearms, tactics at the mock
   town "Hogan's Alley," legal/evidence instruction, physical fitness). VERIFIED-standard, widely
   documented FBI Academy program length.
2. **Special Agent, First Office of Assignment (FOA)** — general criminal squad, 2–3 years before
   eligible for specialized programs.
3. **Certified Undercover Employee (UCE)** — a real FBI designation. Candidates undergo psychological
   screening, background deep-dive, and role-play assessment before certification (Office of Justice
   Programs abstract, "Selection and Training of Undercover Personnel," confirms formal
   testing/screening standards proposed and adopted industry-wide). Cleared for "Group I" operations
   — sensitive/high-risk undercover work requiring sign-off from FBI HQ's Undercover Review Committee
   per the DOJ **Attorney General's Guidelines on FBI Undercover Operations** (real, documented policy
   dating to the 1980s, adopted after undercover-operation controversies).
4. **Deep-cover operative** — embedded long-term inside a target organization; no formal pay-grade
   change from UCE, but risk/duration compounds. This is the "identity erosion" rung — the FBI's own
   undercover-agent psychological literature documents strain on marriages/family life as a
   recognized occupational hazard (OJP abstract, "family life of the candidate is given special
   attention, since undercover work puts great strain on marital and family relationships").
5. **Case agent nearing takedown** — building the prosecutable case (wiretaps/recordings) toward
   indictment; the agent transitions from "friend" back to witness.
6. **Supervisory Special Agent (SSA)** — GS-14 tier; runs squads of agents, including newer UCEs,
   from a desk instead of the field.
7. **Assistant Special Agent in Charge (ASAC) / Special Agent in Charge (SAC)** — GS-15 / Senior
   Executive Service (SES); runs a field office, answers to DOJ and Bureau HQ.
8. **The Bureau above you** — FBI Director (Senate-confirmed, statutory **10-year term** since
   J. Edgar Hoover, Executive Schedule pay) and DOJ oversight; Congress holds oversight hearings on
   Bureau conduct. The apex is institutional, not personal — the file outlives the man.

## Pay spine (ILLUSTRATIVE — GS scale composite, not a specific year's OPM table)

- NAT/early SA: **GS-10**, reported entry-level total compensation (base + mandatory **25% Law
  Enforcement Availability Pay/LEAP** + locality) commonly cited in the **~$58K–$86K** range
  depending on locality (multiple 2026 salary-aggregator sources; treat as approximate, not an exact
  OPM figure).
- Full-performance field agent: **GS-13**, roughly **$100K–$130K** all-in with LEAP/locality.
- SSA: **GS-14**, roughly **$130K–$155K**.
- ASAC/SAC: **GS-15/SES**, roughly **$172K–$187K**.
- FBI Director: Executive Schedule II, historically reported around **$203K** (figure drifts with
  annual Executive Schedule adjustments — flagged approximate).
- **SHARE-WORTHY FACT (reasonably well-supported, not 100%-nailed-down):** public salary reporting
  turned up **no separate "undercover" or hazard-pay differential** for Certified Undercover
  Employees beyond the standard LEAP every Special Agent already receives — the person risking a
  bullet at a made-ceremony is paid on the identical scale as a desk analyst. Framed in-episode as
  "the danger buys you more eyes on you, not more in the check."
- **Mandatory retirement age: 57** for federal law enforcement officers including FBI Special Agents
  (5 U.S.C. §8335(b) — VERIFIED, well-documented statute). No rank, however high, escapes it — a clean
  cyclical/loop-close mechanic: everyone's career has the same hard ceiling built in by law, not
  performance.

## Real mechanics used as grounding (never depicting a real living person as the protagonist)

- **Joseph D. Pistone / "Donnie Brasco"** — the best-documented real case. Infiltrated the Bonanno
  crime family for roughly **six years (1976–1981)**, leading to widely reported **~100+ convictions**
  (exact totals vary slightly by source/era of reporting — treat as an approximate, frequently-cited
  range). The Bonanno family reportedly placed a bounty (commonly cited as **$500,000**) on his life
  after his identity surfaced. Pistone needed a new identity/relocation and has written that the
  operation cost him his marriage under the strain of the double life (per his own memoir, "Donnie
  Brasco: My Undercover Life in the Mafia"). Used ONLY as real background grounding for the genre's
  known stakes — the episode's protagonist, mentor, target, and rival are a **fictional composite**.
- **Undercover Review Committee / Group I vs Group II operations** — the DOJ Attorney General's
  Guidelines distinguish routine (Group II) undercover work from sensitive Group I operations (those
  risking entrapment claims, significant illegal activity by the UC, or corruption of public
  officials), which require FBI HQ committee sign-off. Real, documented policy layer — dramatized as
  "the paperwork that has to clear Washington before you're allowed to become someone else."
- **DOJ Assets Forfeiture Fund** — publicly reported to hold on the order of **billions of dollars**
  in deposits in recent fiscal years (exact annual figures vary and were not independently re-verified
  this run — flagged as illustrative order-of-magnitude, not a cited exact figure for any single year).
  Used as the "evidence money" spine below.

## The spine: EVIDENCE vs. PAYCHECK (a new dual-track flavor)

Distinct from the six prior spine flavors already catalogued (valuation-vs-personal, inverted power-
vs-pay, control-vs-own, scenario pure-money, dual-track-valuation, unbroken-climb-moral-only-reversal):
here the **big gold on-screen number is money that passes through the protagonist's hands as
evidence** (buy money, a skim, a ledger, a seizure total) — money that is never, and can never be,
theirs — while the **small gold sub-caption is the actual GS paycheck**, capped and nearly frozen by
statute regardless of the danger. The drama is the widening gap between what you touch and what you
keep, resolving at the apex into a pension (roughly **40% of high-3 average salary** at mandatory
retirement, a real, standard federal law-enforcement pension mechanic) versus a nationwide forfeiture
total in the billions that was never yours to spend for a single day.

Illustrative escalation (ILLUSTRATIVE dramatized figures unless flagged VERIFIED above):
- L1 Trainee: $0 evidence / ~$58K paycheck (VERIFIED-range GS-10+LEAP)
- L2 Special Agent, FOA: $12,000 (first buy-bust cash) / ~$63K
- L3 Certified UCE, first Group I op: $80,000 (a card-game skim counted as evidence) / ~$71K
- L4 Deep cover: $1.2M (a loan-shark ledger copied) / ~$84K
- L5 Case agent, the wire: $9M (the takedown's seizure warrants) / ~$98K
- L6 SSA: $40M (forfeiture across the squad's now-supervised cases) / ~$115K (GS-14 range)
- L7 ASAC/SAC: $300M+ (a field office's annual forfeiture total) / ~$180K (GS-15/SES range)
- L8 Apex: the Bureau's national forfeiture total (illustrative, order of billions) / a fixed pension
  at mandatory retirement, age 57 — the loop closes on the one number no rank can climb past.
