# Your Life in the Yakuza at Every Rank — Research

POV episode. The viewer climbs a fictional, composite yakuza gumi ("family") from the lowest apprentice
to kumichō, supreme boss of the wider syndicate it belongs to. Framed as a **dramatized cautionary
STORY**, never how-to, and never a depiction of any real, currently active organization or living
individual — the gumi, the syndicate, and every named character (Sato, Kenji, Yumi, Ishii) are
**fictional composites**. Real historical events (the 2015 Yamaguchi-gumi split), real law (the 1992
Bōtaihō, the 2009–2011 exclusion ordinances), and real NPA statistics are used only as verified
background texture/grounding, never attributed to the fictional protagonist as fact.

## The real ladder (documented core structure, elaborated into 8 dramatized rungs for pacing)
Real, well-sourced yakuza hierarchy is an **oyabun–kobun** (parent–child) fictive-kinship pyramid, not
a strict corporate ladder: **kumichō** (supreme boss of a syndicate) → **wakagashira** (the #2,
governs multiple regional gangs) → **shatei-gashira** (a regional/branch boss) → **shatei** (a made,
sworn member) → unranked recruits/apprentices at the bottom [verified core structure — Heart of Souls
wiki citing standard yakuza-studies sources; StudyAround.blog]. This episode elaborates that
4–5-tier documented core into 8 narrative rungs for pacing (an INVENTED elaboration, flagged):
1. **Kobun** — bottom apprentice/prospect; runs errands, no formal sakazuki yet.
2. **Wakashu** — "young one"; collects/moves money for the gumi, still unmade.
3. **Shatei** — a made member, formally sworn via **sakazuki** (see below).
4. **Shatei-gashira** — runs his own small crew/front business under a boss.
5. **Shibuchō** — a branch/district chief controlling several crews.
6. **Wakagashira** — the family's #2, oversees every branch [documented rank].
7. **Oyabun** — boss of your own affiliated gumi under the larger syndicate's umbrella.
8. **Kumichō** — supreme boss of the entire syndicate [documented rank].

## Real mechanics / jargon (woven into POV, never how-to)
- **Sakazuki** — a formal sake-cup exchange ceremony that creates the fictive parent-child bond and
  formalizes rank; different cup rituals mark different bonds (oyabun-kobun, brothers) [verified —
  Heart of Souls wiki; Anthropology Review].
- **Yubitsume** — atonement ritual: amputating the top joint of one's own left little finger to
  apologize for an offense to one's oyabun, origin in Edo-period bakuto gambling houses; repeated
  offenses move to the next joint, then the right hand [verified — Wikipedia "Yubitsume"; NextShark].
- **Irezumi** — the traditional full-body tattoo, historically hand-poked (**tebori**); a complete
  back/body suit commonly runs **200–600 hours** across months or years, prized partly because
  enduring the pain demonstrates discipline and commitment to the group [verified, range — Lighthouse
  Tattoo; multiple tattoo-history sources].
- **Jōnōkin** — the tribute/kickback a member owes up the chain every period; real front-business
  rackets: pachinko parlors, construction and real-estate racketeering, loan-sharking, protection
  money, and historically the docks/waterfront — general, widely documented yakuza revenue streams.
- **Hamon / zetsuen** — formal excommunication from a gumi, publicized so no other gumi will deal with
  the expelled member; a real, standard yakuza practice used to disown members who break ranks.

## Real events used as grounding (not depicted as this episode's literal history)
- **The 2015 Yamaguchi-gumi split**: on Aug 27, 2015, over a dozen factions broke from Japan's largest
  syndicate (leadership friction over dues and advancement) to form the Kobe Yamaguchi-gumi, triggering
  a running gang war — ~2,000 arrests tied to ~100 incidents; a further 2017 splinter (Ninkyō
  Yamaguchi-gumi) made it a three-way standoff; the main factions declared the feud over in April 2025
  [verified — Nippon.com; CNN; SCMP; Wikipedia "Kobe Yamaguchi-gumi"]. This episode's midpoint "the
  split" is a fictionalized, smaller-scale echo of this real, well-documented event — used as background
  grounding for the reversal beat, not a depiction of the actual Yamaguchi-gumi or its real leadership.
- **The Bōtaihō (1992) + prefectural Yakuza Exclusion Ordinances (2009–2011, all 47 prefectures by end
  of 2011)**: ban ordinary citizens/businesses from dealing with designated members — no bank accounts,
  no apartment leases in their own name, hotels/insurers refuse them; a "five-year clause" keeps even
  FORMER members excluded for five years after leaving [verified — LegalClarity; Wikipedia "Yakuza
  exclusion ordinances"]. (The episode's granddaughter/school-enrollment beat was cut from the final
  script for lack of a direct source — the banking/housing/insurance exclusions above are the ones used
  on screen, and they are independently well-sourced.)
- **Membership collapse**: NPA-reported bōryokudan membership+associates peaked at **~184,100 in 1963**
  and has fallen every year since the exclusion-law era began; 2024 NPA data put members+associates at
  **18,800** (20th consecutive annual decline, a new record low), with 2025 figures around **9,400
  members + 8,200 quasi-members (~17,600 total)** [verified — Nippon.com "Established Organized Crime
  Syndicates Continue to Age and Decline"; factually.co; Statista]. Peak-to-2025 decline ≈ **90%**
  ((184,100 − 17,600) / 184,100). This is the episode's central **"wait — that's real?" share beat**.
  Police attention has since shifted toward unaffiliated, tattoo-less, internet-recruited "tokuryū"
  crews precisely because they're harder to track than sworn, sakazuki-bound yakuza [verified — NY1/AP;
  Asia Crime Century].

## The on-screen spine (illustrative $ figures — real yakuza finances are cash-based and never audited;
flagged throughout as DRAMATIZED, not sourced figures)
- **L1 Kobun:** ~$40/WK kozukai (allowance) — essentially nothing, chores and errands only.
- **L2 Wakashu:** ~$400/WK collected and handed upward — none of it kept.
- **L3 Shatei:** ~$2,000/MO personal cut after the real, made-member sakazuki.
- **L4 Shatei-gashira:** ~$18,000/MO laundered through a construction/pachinko front crew.
- **L5 Shibuchō:** ~$60,000/MO moving through a multi-business branch.
- **L6 Wakagashira:** ~$2,400,000/YR crossing his desk (every branch's tribute).
- **L7 Oyabun:** ~$11,000,000/YR gross across his own gumi's legitimate-looking businesses.
- **L8 Kumichō:** ~$340,000,000/YR estimated across the whole syndicate's fronts — **but $0 in his own
  name**: no deed, account, or share certificate a court can seize. "Own nothing, control everything"
  is a real, commonly cited yakuza and organized-crime asset-protection maxim, echoed here.

## Story spine (for the writer)
- **Mentor: Sato** — a mid-level oyabun who recruits the protagonist off the street, sponsors the first
  sakazuki, teaches giri (duty) over ninjō (feeling). Betrayal/loss payoff: refuses to bend to a
  breakaway faction during the midpoint's syndicate split, is hamon'd (excommunicated), then killed —
  the code that made him also unmade him.
- **Rival: Kenji** — a fellow recruit, faster and colder, who sides with the breakaway faction at the
  midpoint reversal and confirms Sato's death to the protagonist — the rival's taunt.
- **Home/want:** Yumi, the protagonist's mother, folds shirts at a dry cleaner, missing two finger
  joints from an old wringer-machine accident, and owes a loan shark $600 by Friday. Concrete want:
  get her out from under that debt. This plants the yubitsume payoff at Level 7 (the protagonist takes
  a joint himself, on purpose, for a debt that isn't his — an ironic echo of Yumi's accidental loss).
- **Sensory/motif anchor:** the sakazuki cup — poured three times across the arc (first joining,
  becoming made, ascending to kumichō) — paired with a body-dread fact, not a feeling ("Your hand
  doesn't shake taking it. That's the part that scares you"), deepening each time it recurs.
- **Anaphora:** "The family buys respect. The family buys protection. The family never once buys you
  out of owing it."
- **Cold open type:** AFTERMATH — the apex-era protagonist just outside a violent night, a detective
  who's built an eleven-year file that's never once held up in court. Structural variation vs. the
  last two produced episodes: lottery_winner used MID-ACTION, self_made_billionaire used
  FLASH-FORWARD — AFTERMATH was last used by crypto_founder, three episodes back (not consecutive).
- **Act-two shape:** an UNBROKEN NUMERIC CLIMB whose only real drop is moral/relational (Sato's death),
  distinct from lottery_winner's spike-crash-rebuild and self_made_billionaire's steady-climb-with-one
  numeric drop.
- **Ending flavor:** TORCH-PASSING — the protagonist, now old, deliberately chooses whether to extend
  the cup to a new kid the way Sato once watched him, rather than a literal same-spot repeat (lottery's
  ending was CYCLICAL/same-spot — this is not consecutive with that).
- **Universe thread (deliberately unresolved):** the detective's eleven-year file, and what happens to
  it after the protagonist becomes kumichō — mentioned, never closed.

## FLAGS / unverified or dramatized
- Personal/family income figures ($40/wk → $340M/yr) are **ILLUSTRATIVE** dramatizations; real yakuza
  income is untaxed cash, never audited, and academic/journalistic estimates for a single syndicate's
  total revenue vary by orders of magnitude. Treat every $ figure on screen as a dramatized estimate.
  The 8-rung ladder is an INVENTED elaboration of the documented 4–5-tier core structure, for pacing.
- Sakazuki, yubitsume, irezumi/tebori (200–600 hrs), jōnōkin, hamon, the 2015 Yamaguchi-gumi split, the
  1992/2009–2011 exclusion laws, and the ~90% membership decline (184,100 peak → ~17,600 in 2025) are
  **verified** per the sources cited above.
- The granddaughter/school-enrollment beat considered for Level 8 was **cut** — no direct source was
  found for yakuza members' children being refused school enrollment specifically, so the episode uses
  only the independently verified bank-account/apartment-lease/insurance exclusions instead.
