# Research: Bernie Madoff / the Madoff Ponzi scheme

Verified 2026-08-17 via web search against SEC releases, DOJ/FBI records, and contemporaneous news
coverage (CNN, NPR, CNBC, FBI.gov, DOJ archive, NBC, Fox, CourthouseNews). Sources cited inline per
fact. Anything not independently confirmed is in the CUT list at the bottom and does NOT appear in
content.py.

## Who

- **Bernard Lawrence "Bernie" Madoff**, born **April 29, 1938**, in **Queens, New York**. [Britannica,
  CNN Fast Facts, Biography.com]
- Founded **Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC** in **1960**, at age 22, with **$5,000** he had
  saved from work as a lifeguard and installing lawn sprinklers. [EBSCO, Biography.com]
- Built it into one of the largest market-making firms on Wall Street and a pioneer of electronic
  trading technology behind NASDAQ. Served as **non-executive chairman of the NASDAQ board of
  directors in 1990, 1991, and 1993.** [EBSCO, CNN]
- The legitimate market-making/trading business occupied the **19th floor** of 885 Third Avenue in
  Manhattan (the "Lipstick Building"). The fraudulent **investment advisory business** — the Ponzi
  scheme — was run separately, one floor below, on the **17th floor**, staffed by no more than **24
  employees** who were walled off from the rest of the firm. [Commercial Observer, Forbes]
- Wife: **Ruth Madoff**. Sons **Mark** and **Andrew Madoff** worked in the legitimate market-making
  side of the firm, not the 17th-floor advisory arm. Brother **Peter Madoff** was chief compliance
  officer and senior managing director. [FBI, NBC]

## The mechanism (verified, plain terms)

Madoff's investment advisory arm told clients their money was invested using a **"split-strike
conversion"** strategy — buying a basket of S&P 100 stocks and using options to collar the gains and
losses — producing steady reported annual returns of roughly 10% or more, in good markets and bad.
[CEO Today, market histories]

In reality, **no trades were happening.** New investor money was used to pay off earlier investors
claiming withdrawals; account statements showing trades and returns were fabricated on the 17th
floor. This is the classic Ponzi mechanism: it requires an ever-growing flow of new money to keep
paying out old money, and it collapses the moment redemptions outrun new deposits. [SEC OIG report;
FBI]

- **Frank DiPascali**, the firm's chief financial/operating officer on the 17th floor, later pleaded
  guilty (2009) and testified the trade statements were **"all fake."** He died in 2015 before he
  could be sentenced. [NBC]
- **David Friehling**, of the three-person accounting firm **Friehling & Horowitz**, based in a strip
  mall in New City, NY, produced the audits that let the fraud pass for decades. He later pleaded
  guilty. [Time, Berkeley Law]

## The whistleblower the SEC ignored

- **Harry Markopolos**, a financial analyst, tried and failed to replicate Madoff's returns in the
  late 1990s and concluded the numbers were mathematically impossible. He submitted written warnings
  to the SEC in **1999, 2000, 2001, 2005, 2007, and 2008.** [multiple sources cross-confirming]
- His most detailed submission, a 2005 memo titled **"The World's Largest Hedge Fund Is a Fraud,"**
  laid out 29 red flags. [Alexander Jarvis archive, ACFE]
- The SEC's response, according to its own 2009 Inspector General report, was limited and inadequate —
  investigators largely just asked Madoff directly whether he was running a Ponzi scheme and accepted
  his answer. The SEC never independently verified a single trade with a custodian or clearing house.
  [SEC OIG report, NBC]

## The collapse

- The 2008 financial crisis triggered a wave of client redemption requests — roughly **$7 billion** in
  withdrawal demands by some estimates — that Madoff could not cover with new money coming in.
- In early December 2008, Madoff told his sons Mark and Andrew that the investment business was
  **"one big lie"** — a Ponzi scheme. [PBS, WCNC]
- His sons reported him to federal authorities on **December 10, 2008.** He was arrested by the FBI
  the next morning, **December 11, 2008**, at his Manhattan apartment. [FBI.gov, CBS]
- FBI Special Agent Ted Cacioppi asked Madoff if there was an innocent explanation. Madoff's verified
  reply: **"There is no innocent explanation. I've been running a massive Ponzi scheme."**
  [MarketWatch, citing released FBI 302 files]
- The SEC's civil complaint, filed the same week, alleged a fraud of roughly **$50 billion** — the
  figure known at the time of the arrest, before a full accounting. [SEC press release 2008-293]
- A fuller accounting later put the **fictitious value shown on client statements at $64.8 billion**
  (paper gains that never existed) — the figure most commonly cited as "the Madoff fraud." Trustee
  Irving Picard's own accounting of **actual cash losses** — money genuinely invested and never
  returned, not counting fake paper profits — was about **$17.5 billion.** [multiple sources; D&O
  Diary; NYU Law]

## Charges, plea, sentence

- **March 12, 2009**: Madoff pleaded guilty to all **11 federal felony counts** — including securities
  fraud, wire fraud, mail fraud, money laundering, perjury, and false filings with the SEC. There was
  no trial; he admitted everything. [DOJ SDNY]
- **June 29, 2009**: Sentenced by U.S. District Judge Denny Chin to **150 years** in federal prison —
  the maximum possible, and effectively a life sentence at age 71. [FBI, CNN Money, Courthouse News]
- Verified quotes from his sentencing allocution: **"I am sorry. I know it will not help you."** /
  **"I left a legacy of shame. It is something I will live with for the rest of my life."** / **"I live
  in a tormented state for all the pain and suffering I created."** [CNBC, Daily Beast]
- Judge Chin called the crimes **"extraordinarily evil"** and said the fraud "was not merely a
  bloodless crime that takes place on paper but one that takes a staggering human toll." [Courthouse
  News]
- **June 2012**: Brother **Peter Madoff** pleaded guilty to conspiracy and falsifying records; sentenced
  **December 20, 2012**, to **10 years** in prison and forced to forfeit a symbolic **$143.1 billion.**
  [DOJ SDNY, NBC, Forbes]

## Aftermath

- **December 11, 2010** — exactly two years to the day after his father's arrest — **Mark Madoff**
  died by suicide in his Manhattan apartment, amid ongoing clawback lawsuits from the trustee. [NBC]
- Trustee **Irving Picard** pursued "net winners" — investors who had withdrawn more than they put in
  — for the return of fraudulent profits. By his own accounting, he recovered/distributed roughly
  **$14+ billion** toward the $17.5 billion in real losses; combined with a separate DOJ Madoff Victim
  Fund, total recoveries reached about **93–94%** of verified losses by the time the process wound
  down around 2024. [NYU Law, CNN 2024]
- Known feeder fund **Fairfield Greenwich Group** funneled about **$7.2 billion** to Madoff and
  collected hundreds of millions in fees; its own "due diligence" largely consisted of accepting
  Madoff's fabricated paperwork. [Wikipedia/Fairfield Greenwich, propublica reference]
- The **Elie Wiesel Foundation for Humanity** lost virtually its entire endowment, reported at about
  **$15.2 million.** [propublica-referenced reporting]
- Madoff reported having stage-4 kidney disease by January 2014. He died in federal prison on
  **April 14, 2021**, at age **82.** [CNBC, PBS]
