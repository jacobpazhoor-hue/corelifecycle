# WeWork — research notes (verified via web search, 2026-09-01)

Subject: WeWork Inc., co-founder/CEO Adam Neumann. Coworking real-estate company that reached a
$47 billion private valuation, collapsed its own IPO in six weeks in 2019 by publishing an S-1 that
exposed its finances and governance, was rescued by SoftBank, went public via SPAC in 2021 at a
fraction of its peak value, and filed Chapter 11 bankruptcy in November 2023.

No named person in this story was criminally charged, convicted, or sentenced — this is a
governance/business-failure story, not a fraud prosecution. No R1/R4-style open-legal-status claims
apply. Write accordingly: "resigned," "stepped down," "gave up the bid," never "convicted"/"pleaded"/
"sentenced" about anyone here.

## Timeline (verified, with sources)

- **2006** — Adam Neumann, then 27, ran a startup called **Krawlers**, selling baby clothes/onesies
  with built-in knee pads for crawling infants. It merged with another baby-apparel firm, Baby Egg,
  and Neumann set up shop in Brooklyn's Dumbo neighborhood — introduced to the space by architect
  friend **Miguel McKelvey**. [moviemaker.com, lovemoney.com — "WeCrashed: Did Adam Neumann Really
  Try to Sell Knee Pads for Babies?"]
- **2008** — Neumann and McKelvey ran an earlier eco-conscious coworking space, GreenDesk, in
  Brooklyn, before selling their stake and starting WeWork. [Britannica]
- **2010** — WeWork founded by Adam Neumann and Miguel McKelvey. First location opened in Manhattan's
  SoHo district, backed by a $15 million commitment from an early investor. [Britannica; Wikipedia]
- **2014–2016** — Rapid, capital-intensive international expansion; valuation rose from **$1.4
  billion to $10.5 billion**. [restructuringnewsletter.com / Pari Passu deep dive]
- **2017** — SoftBank's **Masayoshi Son** visited WeWork's New York HQ for about 30 minutes and
  committed **$4.4 billion** through the SoftBank Vision Fund. [Fast Company "WeFail"; CNBC]
- **January 2019** — Company rebranded "**The We Company**." SoftBank led a Series H round valuing
  the company at **$47 billion**. [CNBC; multiple]
- **August 14, 2019** — The We Company filed its **S-1** registration statement with the SEC ahead of
  a planned IPO, at an implied valuation around $47 billion. Key disclosures:
  - **2018:** revenue **$1.82 billion**, net loss **$1.93 billion**.
  - **First half of 2019:** net loss **>$900 million** on revenue of **$1.54 billion**.
  - **528 locations** (up from 485 at the end of Q1 2019); ~56% of revenue from the U.S., ~50% of
    memberships outside the U.S.; enterprise customers were 40% of memberships.
  - The filing used a non-GAAP metric called "**Community Adjusted EBITDA**," which excluded costs
    like marketing, G&A and development — an invented profit measure critics mocked immediately.
  - The filing stated: **"Our mission is to elevate the world's consciousness."**
  - It disclosed that WeWork had paid **$5.9 million** in partnership interests for rights to the
    "We" trademark to an LLC **controlled by Adam Neumann** — later returned by Neumann amid the
    backlash. [PitchBook: "Adam Neumann gives $5.9M trademark payment back to WeWork"]
  - It disclosed a dual/triple-class share structure: Class A common stock carried 1 vote/share while
    Neumann's high-vote "Founder" shares (Class B/C) carried **20 votes per share**, with no sunset
    provision. Amid the governance backlash, WeWork amended the filing to cut the high-vote stock
    from 20 votes/share to **10 votes/share**. [CNBC "WeWork makes sweeping corporate governance
    changes ahead of IPO," Sept 13 2019]
  - [CNBC "WeWork releases S-1 filing for IPO"; Crunchbase News "WeWork Files Its S-1"; Wolf Street]
- **The six-week valuation collapse** — Marketed off the $47 billion January 2019 round, the IPO
  roadshow's revised guidance reportedly fell as low as **$10-12 billion**, and by the time the
  filing was withdrawn on September 30 bankers were discussing a SoftBank rescue valuing the firm
  around **$7.5-8 billion** — a drop of roughly **$38-40 billion** from the $47 billion peak inside
  the six weeks between the August 14 S-1 filing and the September 30 withdrawal. [The Conversation,
  "Fallout from WeWork's failed IPO shows the folly of excessive valuations"; contemporaneous CNBC/
  Reuters coverage of the collapsing valuation guidance]
- **Lease term vs. membership term** — WeWork's own disclosed average lease commitment ran about
  **15 years**, while the average member membership term was reported around **19 months** and
  WeWork's standard membership terms allow cancellation on short notice (WeWork's published
  membership terms cite a 5-day notice ahead of a commitment term's end, with 30 days' notice used
  for fee changes) — the "years of fixed rent against a walk-away-anytime membership" gap the
  episode names. [WeWork membership agreement terms; Not So Deep Dive: WeWork Stock (Ticker: WE)]
- **September 24, 2019** — Adam Neumann **resigned as CEO** under investor and board pressure.
  [multiple, incl. idealsvdr.com]
- **September 30, 2019** — The We Company formally **withdrew** its S-1 IPO filing. [CNBC "WeWork
  says it will file request to withdraw its IPO prospectus," Axios, Fortune]
- **Late 2019** — SoftBank stepped in with a rescue package worth roughly **$9.5 billion**. Neumann's
  own exit package was reported at up to **$1.7 billion total**, made up of roughly **$970 million**
  for his shares, a **$185 million "consulting fee,"** and **$500 million in credit** to help repay
  personal loans. [idealsvdr.com; As You Sow]
- **November 21, 2019** — WeWork announced it would lay off **2,400 employees** (about 19% of its
  workforce) as part of the SoftBank-led restructuring. [CNBC "WeWork lays off 2,400 employees";
  NBC News; Guardian/Pressreader]
- **May 2020** — On a SoftBank earnings call, **Masayoshi Son** said: **"We made a failure on
  investing in WeWork and I've been admitting that several times I was foolish."** He also said,
  separately, **"I was wrong."** SoftBank marked its WeWork stake down to **$2.9 billion** (from
  $7.3 billion at the end of 2019, and $47 billion at its 2019 peak). SoftBank's cumulative
  investment in WeWork was reported around **$18.5 billion**. [PYMNTS "SoftBank CEO Says WeWork
  Investment Was 'Foolish'"; CNBC "SoftBank values WeWork at $2.9 billion, down from $47 billion a
  year ago"]
- **March 25, 2021** — WeWork announced a deal to go public via a SPAC merger with **BowX
  Acquisition Corp.**, valuing WeWork at an initial enterprise value of about **$9 billion**. [SEC
  filings; CNBC; Commercial Observer]
- **October 20–21, 2021** — The merger closed and WeWork began trading on the **NYSE** under ticker
  **"WE"** on October 21, 2021; shares jumped more than 13% on debut. [CNBC "WeWork shares jump more
  than 13% in public markets debut after SPAC merger"]
- **August 2022** — Venture firm **Andreessen Horowitz (a16z)** invested **$350 million** in
  Neumann's new residential real-estate startup, **Flow**, valuing it at **$1 billion before it had
  launched** — reported as a16z's largest individual check to date. [TechCrunch "a16z says 'WeBack'
  to WeWork's Neumann with its biggest check ever"]
- **August 2023** — WeWork issued a "going concern" warning about its ability to continue operating.
  [Fortune: "WeWork: $47 billion to $270 million — a 'going concern'"]
- **November 6, 2023** — WeWork Inc. and **517 affiliated entities** filed **Chapter 11** bankruptcy
  petitions in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the **District of New Jersey**. The filing listed more
  than **$18.6 billion** of debt against more than **$15 billion** of assets. The U.S. Chapter 11
  filing did not cover WeWork's locations outside the U.S. and Canada. CEO **David Tolley** said
  about 90% of the company's lenders had agreed to convert **$3 billion** of debt into equity.
  [TechCrunch "WeWork, once worth $47 billion, files for bankruptcy"; CNBC; Epiq11 case site;
  Mayer Brown / Orrick legal summaries]
- **March 2024** — Adam Neumann, backed by Flow, made a bid (reported around **$500 million**, with
  some reporting describing an offer up to $650 million across the process) to buy WeWork back out of
  bankruptcy. [Forbes "Why Adam Neumann's Reported $500 Million WeWork Bid Could Easily Fail";
  CoStar]
- **Spring 2024** — Neumann **abandoned** his bid to reacquire WeWork. He called the bankruptcy
  "disappointing" in public remarks. [PYMNTS "Co-Founder Adam Neumann Abandons Attempt to Buy
  Bankrupt WeWork"; Fox Business]
- **June 11, 2024** — WeWork officially **emerged from Chapter 11** as a private company. The
  reorganization eliminated more than **$4 billion** of debt, roughly halved future lease
  liabilities (saving an estimated **$12 billion** in present value), and raised about **$450
  million** in new capital. [The Real Deal "Judge approves WeWork bankruptcy exit"; Davis Polk
  case summary]

## Culture detail (flagged, sourced to reporting on litigation/former-employee accounts — used
sparingly, framed as reported rather than as flat undisputed fact)
- Former employees told Business Insider that Adam Neumann was frequently seen barefoot in the
  office and demanded cases of **Don Julio 1942 tequila** (~$140/bottle) at WeWork locations he
  visited, with music played at party volume. [aol.com/Business Insider aggregation]
- A gender-discrimination lawsuit against WeWork included allegations, reported by outlets covering
  the suit (Dealbreaker/Business Insider), that Neumann drank tequila and smoked marijuana barefoot
  aboard the company's private jet. Presented in the episode, if at all, as an allegation from that
  lawsuit — not stated as flat fact.
