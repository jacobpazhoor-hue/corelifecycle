## The real ladder (composite, elaborated to 8 rungs for pacing — flagged)

The Mongol army/empire had a real, documented decimal command structure (arban/jaghun/minghan/tumen)
plus an administrative layer above it (noyan governors, khans of the four khanates, the Great Khan/
Khagan). No single historical person literally held all 8 rungs in one lifetime — this episode
compresses ~1206–1271 (Genghis Khan's unification through Kublai Khan's Yuan founding) into one
composite POV climb, the same mythic-compression the existing ROMAN and SAMURAI history-format packs
use (a recruit doesn't literally become Caesar/shogun in one human lifetime either).

1. **Herder's son** — pre-army steppe life, Genghis Khan (Temüjin) still unifying the tribes (pre-1206).
2. **Arban** (10 men, one commander) — VERIFIED: the base unit; deliberately mixed across tribes to
   break clan loyalty and build loyalty to the state instead (Ancient Origins / mongolianz.com).
3. **Jaghun commander** (100 men — 10 arbans).
4. **Minghan commander** (1,000 men — 10 jaghuns) — VERIFIED structure via mingghan-u noyan.
5. **Tumen commander / noyan** (10,000 men — 10 minghans), the top field-command rank — VERIFIED
   (Wikipedia "Mingghan"; Grokipedia "Tumen (unit)").
6. **Regional noyan / governor** — administers a conquered province for the Khagan: taxation, the Yam
   relay, tribute in silver.
7. **Khan of a khanate** — after the empire splits (see Midpoint below), rules one of the four
   successor khanates (Golden Horde / Chagatai / Ilkhanate / Yuan) as a sovereign in his own right.
8. **Khagan (Great Khan)** — the apex title; after 1260 only the Yuan-based line (Kublai) still holds
   it, and the other three khanates no longer answer to it in practice (Division of the Mongol Empire,
   Wikipedia).

## Verified facts used as story grounding

- **Decimal army**: arban(10)→jaghun(100)→minghan(1,000)→tumen(10,000), each level with its own
  commander (arban-noyon / jaghun-noyad / mingghan-u noyad / tumen-noyad). Deliberately mixed-tribe
  composition to erase clan loyalty. [Ancient Origins; mongolianz.com; Wikipedia "Mingghan"]
- **Collective punishment (Yassa)**: units were mutually accountable — desertion/cowardice by one
  man could mean punishment for the whole arban, which the state treated as a family unit, "as loyally
  as brothers." [jamogrand.com; mongolianz.com — the exact scope of "punishment" varies by source;
  used here as documented military discipline, not a specific literal case]
- **The Khwarazmian campaign (1219–1221)**: triggered by the governor of Otrar (Inalchuq), acting
  under Shah Muhammad II, massacring a ~450-person Mongol trade caravan — Genghis Khan's answer was
  total war: Otrar, Bukhara, Samarkand, Urgench sacked in succession. The chronicler Juvaini attributes
  to Genghis Khan the line "I am the punishment of God" at Bukhara — FLAG: this is a chronicle
  attribution (written decades later), not a verbatim contemporary transcript; used here only as
  textured dialogue-adjacent color, not asserted as a literal quote in the episode.
- **Scale of conquest deaths**: estimates for the whole Mongol conquest era (1206–1405) range roughly
  20–60 million; the popular "40 million" figure traces to a criticized 19th/20th-century population
  atlas using unreliable Chinese census data; conservative modern scholarship clusters ~35–37 million.
  FLAG: disputed, wide range — used only as an order-of-magnitude story beat, never a precise on-screen
  number. [historyatnormandale.wordpress.com; signaldocker.com]
- **Empire scale at peak**: ~23–24 million km² (~9–9.27 million sq mi), the largest contiguous land
  empire in history — roughly 16% of Earth's total land area; commonly cited estimate that close to a
  quarter of the world's population lived under Mongol rule at the empire's height. [Statista;
  historycooperative.org; Britannica]
- **The Yam relay system**: VERIFIED — relay stations every ~20–30 miles, each stocked with 200–400
  fresh horses; authorized riders (carrying a *paiza*, a passport tablet) covered 200–300 km
  (~120–190 mi) per day — for scale, six centuries later the American Pony Express maxed out at ~75
  miles/day. [mongolianz.com; Wikipedia "Yam (route)"; tldrhistory.com]
- **Möngke Khan's death (Aug 11, 1259)** sparked the **Toluid Civil War** (1260–1264) between his
  brothers Kublai and Ariq Böke — VERIFIED. Ariq Böke surrendered at Shangdu, Aug 21, 1264; Kublai
  founded the Yuan dynasty in 1271. The war is what let the four khanates (Golden Horde, Chagatai,
  Ilkhanate, Yuan) become de facto independent — they never fully reunited under one Khagan again.
  [Wikipedia "Möngke Khan", "Ariq Böke", "Division of the Mongol Empire"]
- **Kublai's paper money (chao)**: VERIFIED — the world's first state fiat currency at this scale,
  made from mulberry-bark pulp, sealed with the Khan's stamp. Marco Polo documented it directly:
  refusing chao in payment, or counterfeiting it, was a **capital offense** — punishable by death,
  printed on the note itself. Kublai backed this by confiscating gold/silver bullion, including from
  foreign traders. **This is the episode's ONE share-worthy "wait — that's real?" beat.**
  [historyofinformation.com; fee.org; Marco Polo primary account via multiple secondary sources]
- **Balish/sukhe silver ingots**: VERIFIED as the empire's standardized long-distance unit of account
  (Möngke established a common silver-ingot measure for cross-khanate exchange), but this episode's
  specific per-rank silver figures are ILLUSTRATIVE — no source in this research pass gives an exact
  universal balish weight/value; commonly cited approximations (~2kg silver) exist but are NOT firmly
  sourced here, so the episode keeps silver figures round and clearly dramatized rather than citing a
  false-precision exchange rate. [Cambridge History of the Mongol Empire, ch. "Economic Exchange" —
  abstract only, full text not accessed]

## The $ / wealth escalation spine (illustrative, grounded in the khubi plunder-share system —
NOT a payroll record; flagged as dramatized)

1. Herder's son: family herd of **11 sheep, 2 sick** — subsistence, one bad winter from starving.
2. Arban warrior: first khubi (loot-share) after a raid — **3 silver coins + a felt coat**.
3. Jaghun commander (100): a captured horse-herd share — **40 sheep, a string of 12 horses**.
4. Minghan commander (1,000), Khwarazmian campaign: a sacked-city plunder share — **silk bolts +
   raw silver**, on-screen as **~600 silver dirhams** (Khwarazmian silver coinage, period-accurate unit).
5. Tumen commander/noyan (10,000): province-scale khubi — **quoted in balish (silver ingots)**,
   dramatized as **"400 balish a year passes through your hands"**.
6. Regional governor: **controls tax/tribute intake** for the Khagan — dramatized **"11,000 balish a
   year — you keep 300."** (the "own nothing, control everything" beat, echoing the series' recurring
   wealth-at-the-top motif).
7. Khan of a khanate (post-split): a quarter of the empire's former tribute base — dramatized apex
   number for the khanate's annual revenue.
8. Khagan: the whole Yuan treasury's chao issuance — dramatized as an apex number, undercut by the
   twist that the other 3 khanates' revenue no longer reports to you at all — the "own the title,
   not the empire" cost.

## Characters (fictional composite — no real historical figure is depicted as the protagonist)

- **You** — unnamed POV protagonist, a composite herder's son who rises through the ranks.
- **Chuluun** — mentor, an aging arban commander who takes you in after your family's herd is raided;
  teaches the Yassa's code of loyalty over blood-tribe. Dies at the midpoint reversal (the succession
  war), the betrayal/loss payoff.
- **Ganbaatar** — rival recruit, faster and more ruthless; backs Ariq Böke in the civil war (the
  historical loser), taunts you at the reversal, later a rival khan you must outlast.

  referenced only as background/grounding for real, independently documented events — never given
  invented dialogue or a fictionalized personal arc.
