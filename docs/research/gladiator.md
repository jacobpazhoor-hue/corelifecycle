## The real ladder (composite, 8 rungs for pacing — flagged)

No single historical gladiator literally climbed all 8 rungs (most died in their 20s after 5-34
fights — see mortality below); this compresses the real, documented career path + a step into
ownership/imperial patronage, the same mythic-compression the ROMAN/SAMURAI/MONGOL history packs use.

1. **Novicius** — a fresh entrant to the ludus (gladiator school): war captive, condemned criminal
   (damnati), or slave bought at market. VERIFIED: entrants were purchased/assigned property of the
   lanista (school owner/manager). [Canus Romulus; Rome Wiki "Gladiators"]
2. **Tiro** — has finished training but not yet fought his first bout; becomes a "veteran" only after
   surviving his first fight. VERIFIED terminology. [TheCollector; Rome Wiki]
3. **Quartus/Tertius palus** — the lower two of the four *palus* (grade/rank) tiers gladiators climbed
   through by win record, one ladder per weapon style (murmillo, retiarius, thraex, secutor, etc.).
   VERIFIED: *palus* ranking system, quartus = smallest number of victories. [TheCollector]
4. **Secundus palus** — an established, recognized name in the ludus's roster.
5. **Primus palus** — VERIFIED: the top grade per weapon style — the most successful, most famous
   gladiators in a ludus. [TheCollector]
6. **Free man (earned the rudis) who fights on as a paid auctoratus, OR a doctore (trainer)** —
   VERIFIED: after ~30 contests or ~5 years' service, a surviving gladiator could be awarded the
   *rudis*, a wooden training sword symbolizing discharge from the sacramentum oath. Many star
   gladiators — Flamma among them (below) — were OFFERED the rudis and refused it, choosing to keep
   fighting for money and fame instead. Retired gladiators very commonly became doctores (trainers)
   at a ludus, using the exact skills that kept them alive. [Reliks "The Roman Rudis"; Wulflund;
   ancienthistorylists.com on Flamma]
7. **Lanista** — owns/runs a ludus; buys, trains, and rents out gladiators to editores (the sponsors
   who stage games) for a fee. VERIFIED role and economics. [Colosseum Rome Tickets; Canus Romulus]
8. **Editor (games-sponsor) / emperor above you** — funds and stages the *munera* (games) at whatever
   scale his treasury allows, up to the emperor himself. VERIFIED apex example: Emperor Commodus
   (r. 180-192 AD) personally fought in the arena as a gladiator (staged, against outmatched or
   disarmed opponents) and was paid an enormous sum per appearance from the imperial treasury — a
   scandal to the Senate at the time. He was assassinated in 192 AD by his own wrestling partner,
   Narcissus, strangled in his bath — the ultimate "the top of the ladder isn't safe either" grounding
   fact, referenced only as background, never as this episode's literal protagonist. [Cassius Dio;
   Herodian — standard ancient-history consensus]

## Verified facts used as story grounding

- **Survival odds, per fight**: historian Georges Ville's tally of 1st-century recorded bouts found
  ~19 of 100 fights ended in a death — roughly a 9.5% chance of dying in any single match (i.e. about
  9 in 10 fighters walked away from a given bout). Estimates suggest 80-85% of *defeated* gladiators
  were actually spared, not executed. [Medium/Peter Sikabonyi "Statistical Reality of Roman
  Gladiators"; neutralhistory.com]
- **Survival odds, across a career**: despite decent single-fight odds, few gladiators survived more
  than ~10 bouts; most died between ages 20-30, typically after 5-34 fights. Average age at death per
  gladiator headstones: ~27 (likely skewed older — many who died younger got no headstone at all).
  Gladiators fought only ~2-3 times a year (not weekly) — spectacle, not attrition, was the business
  model, but the risk compounds hard over a multi-year career. [warriorsandlegends.com; the "27"
  figure is the episode's SHARE-WORTHY "wait — that's real?" stat.]
- **The palus grading system**: quartus (lowest) through primus (top) palus, ranked by win record,
  one ladder per weapon specialty. [TheCollector]
- **The rudis**: a wooden sword awarded on discharge (~30 fights or ~5 years' service); freed the
  gladiator from the sacramentum oath. The single most famous verified rudis story: at the Colosseum's
  own inaugural games (80 AD), the poet Martial records a pair — **Priscus and Verus** — who fought so
  evenly and so bravely that Emperor Titus awarded BOTH men the rudis simultaneously, an almost
  unheard-of double reprieve. VERIFIED via Martial's *De Spectaculis* (contemporary primary source).
  This episode borrows the name "Priscus" for the mentor doctore as a grounding nod, without claiming
  he is literally that historical man. [Reliks; multiple classical sources on Martial's epigram]
- **Flamma**: a Syrian-born gladiator (fl. under Hadrian, 117-138 AD) whose tombstone survives —
  34 fights, 21 wins, 9 draws, 4 losses, over ~13 years. VERIFIED: he was offered the rudis (freedom)
  FOUR separate times and refused every one, choosing to keep fighting. Real grounding for this
  episode's Level 6 refusal beat. [ancienthistorylists.com; Ancient Origins]
- **Marcus Attilius**: a free-born Roman citizen who volunteered as an auctoratus (a *tiro*, first
  bout) and upset Hilarus, a seasoned imperial gladiator with 12 wins in 14 fights — VERIFIED via a
  surviving Pompeii graffito recording the match. Grounding for the Level 2 "first bout" beat.
  [Costs-of-Munera source; Pompeii epigraphy scholarship]
- **Diet — "hordearii" (barley-men)**: gladiators trained on a mostly plant/grain diet (barley,
  beans, dried fruit) and deliberately maintained a layer of subcutaneous fat — bioarchaeological
  isotope/bone studies (the Ephesus gladiator graveyard) confirm this; the fat cushioned cuts, reduced
  visible blood loss for showmanship, and protected vital organs without slowing footwork much. This
  is the episode's **sensory anchor motif**: the smell/taste of barley gruel, re-triggered at every
  level — humiliating at the bottom, ritual at the middle, and still exactly the same bowl at the top,
  because the body that keeps you famous is not yours to feed as you please. [widely corroborated
  bioarchaeological consensus, e.g. Fabian Kanz & Karl Grossschmidt's Ephesus study]
- **Pay structure**: gladiators split fight purses with their lanista — commonly cited split is roughly
  20% to an enslaved gladiator, 25% to a free auctoratus, the rest to the lanista who owns/manages
  them. Crowds also threw coins/jewelry directly to a victor as a spontaneous bonus. [neutralhistory.com
  "The Pay of Roman Gladiators"]
- **Re-enlistment fee cap**: Emperor Marcus Aurelius (r. 161-180 AD), tightening costs, capped the fee
  a lanista could charge an editor to re-enlist a top auctoratus at **12,000 sestertii** — VERIFIED,
  a real edict. Used as this episode's Level 5 apex per-appearance figure. [multiple classical-history
  sources on Marcus Aurelius's gladiatorial cost reforms]
- **Munera cost tiers**: a mid-size games budget of 100,000-150,000 sestertii bought five ranks of
  gladiators priced 12,000 / 10,000 / 7,000 / 6,000 / 5,000 sestertii per top fighter in each rank —
  VERIFIED, a Roman legal/economic source on munera costs. Used for the Level 7 lanista figure.
  [pressbooks.bccampus.ca "Costs of Munera and Other Spectacles"]
- **Imperial-scale games**: Emperor Trajan's games celebrating the Dacian conquest (107 AD) reportedly
  ran 123 days and involved roughly 10,000 gladiators and 11,000 animals — FLAG: this figure comes down
  to us via Jerome's Chronicle, itself epitomizing Cassius Dio (a later secondary compression, not a
  contemporary ledger), so treat as an order-of-magnitude ancient claim, not an audited count. Used,
  clearly dramatized, as the Level 8 apex figure — a games budget bigger than most conquered
  provinces' annual tribute. [standard ancient-history citation of Cassius Dio via Jerome]
- **Colosseum inaugural games (80 AD)**: Emperor Titus's dedication games reportedly ran 100 days with
  thousands of animals killed — same secondary-source caveat as above (Cassius Dio). Referenced only
  as texture, never as a precise on-screen number.

## The $ / value escalation spine (illustrative where flagged — this is the SPINE FLAVOR: "PRICE OF A
MAN vs. WINS-TO-FREEDOM" — a new flavor distinct from the six prior spine types already catalogued.
The big gold overlay tracks your MARKET VALUE — what the lanista could sell you for, what an editor
pays to rent you — money that is never legally yours to spend; a parallel sub-caption tracks your
win-count toward the 30-fight rudis threshold, which perversely stalls the more famous (and more
profitable to keep alive and fighting) you become.)

1. Novicius: bought at the block for **600 sestertii** — "less than the price of a good horse"
   (a period-plausible slave/captive purchase price, dramatized; on-screen as your own price tag).
2. Tiro, first bout survived (the Attilius-grounded beat): a **62-sestertii** purse-share (20% cut of
   a small opening-bill purse). Wins so far: 1/30.
3. Quartus/tertius palus: crowd tosses + a modest booking fee, **~500 sestertii** a fight. Wins: 6/30.
4. Secundus palus: the lanista starts quoting your MARKET VALUE to editores directly — **~3,000
   sestertii** to book you for a show. Wins: 14/30.
5. Primus palus (the champion — COLD OPEN level): the Marcus-Aurelius-capped re-enlistment fee, the
   real historical ceiling — **12,000 sestertii** a single appearance. Wins: 22/30 — eight fights from
   the rudis, and the lanista has never had a better reason to keep renewing your contract.
6. Free man (rudis earned, refused — the Flamma-grounded beat): market value keeps climbing on paper
   (a "priceless" headline fighter) but the WINS counter freezes at will — you keep fighting anyway.
7. Lanista (you now own a ludus): the business-scale number — **100,000-150,000 sestertii** per show
   budget flows through your hands; you supply the men, you take the operator's cut.
8. Editor/emperor above you: the apex, dramatized off Trajan's 123-day Dacian games — **an empire's
   treasury, one line-item, 10,000 men, 123 days** — undercut by the emperor's own real fate (Commodus,
   above): the top of this ladder is not immune to the sand either.

## Characters (fictional composite — no real historical figure depicted as the protagonist)

- **You** — unnamed POV protagonist, a composite novicius who rises through the ranks.
- **Priscus** — mentor doctore (trainer), a name borrowed from the real Colosseum-inaugural pair
  (Priscus & Verus, Martial's *De Spectaculis*) as grounding texture, not literally that man. Trains
  you, teaches the barley-and-footwork discipline that keeps fighters alive. Dies at the midpoint
  reversal.
- **Barca** — rival novicius-turned-primus-palus (a real, period-attested Punic/gladiator-style name,
  "lightning"), faster and more ruthless; the taunt at the midpoint, later a rival lanista you must
  outlast.
- Real historical figures referenced only as background grounding, never given invented dialogue or a
  fictionalized personal arc: Flamma, Marcus Attilius, Hilarus, Emperor Titus, Emperor Marcus Aurelius,
  Emperor Trajan, Emperor Commodus.
