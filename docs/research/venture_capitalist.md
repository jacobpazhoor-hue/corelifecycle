# Research — Your Life as a Venture Capitalist at Every Level

Format: **rank**. POV episode. The viewer climbs an institutional VC firm from unpaid-opinion analyst
to the general partner of a mega-fund, then to the LPs (pensions/endowments/sovereign wealth) who
decide which funds get to exist. Web-verified July 2026; FLAGGED items are directional/composite.

## The spine on screen: CAPITAL YOU CAN DEPLOY (power) vs. what you actually KEEP (sub-caption)

Mirrors the hedge-fund/spy "inverted spine" pattern: the big gold number is the **size of the check
you have the authority to write** (or, once you run a fund, the **fund/AUM you command**); the
**sub-caption** carries personal pay + carry — which stays small and mostly *unrealized paper* for
years, because carried interest doesn't pay out until portfolio companies actually exit (a fund's
life runs 7–10+ years). The drama is the gap between the money you move and the money that's real.

## The 8-level ladder + numbers

1. **Analyst** — sources deals (cold outreach, conferences, "deal flow"), builds models, no vote in
   the partner meeting. **Check authority: $0.** All-in pay **~$100–160K** (median total comp ~$117–129K
   incl. bonus; top-tier firms pay analyst base >$160K). Carry, if granted at all, is a token **~1.1%**
   of the firm's carry pool — unvested, won't pay for a decade if ever. Overlay: **$0 · ~$120K PAY**.
2. **Associate** — runs full diligence, presents to partners, may get sign-off to recommend a small
   check. **~$250K** check authority (with a partner's signature). All-in pay **~$160–260K** (post-MBA
   associates at top firms: $200–260K total cash). Carry **0.1–0.5%** of the pool. Overlay:
   **$250,000 · ~$200K PAY**.
3. **Principal / VP** — leads a deal to a signed term sheet, takes a **board OBSERVER seat** (watches,
   can't vote fiduciarily). **~$3,000,000** check authority (a full seed/Series A lead). Total pay
   **~$250–400K** (avg ~$252K) — carry jumps hard here, **3–8%** of the pool, the first allocation that
   could actually matter in ten years. Overlay: **$3,000,000 · ~$300K PAY**.
4. **Partner** — full vote at Investment Committee; the board seat becomes a **board DIRECTOR** seat —
   real fiduciary duty, can be sued alongside the founder. **~$15,000,000** check authority (leads
   growth-stage rounds). Cash comp **~$250–500K+**, plus real carry stacked across multiple fund
   vintages. Overlay: **$15,000,000 · CARRY (UNREALIZED)**.
5. **General Partner** — allocates across the whole fund, personally fundraises from LPs, and must
   make a **GP commitment** — real personal capital into the fund, median **~1.5–1.7%** of fund size
   (funds with 3%+ GP commits outperformed sub-1% peers by ~280bps net IRR over 10 years — LPs read
   this as a confidence signal). On a **$300,000,000** fund that's **~$4,500,000 of your own money,
   locked up for a decade.** Overlay: **$300,000,000 FUND · $4.5M OF IT IS YOURS**.
6. **Found Your Own Fund** — you raise **Fund I**, say **$150,000,000**, and charge **"2 and 20"**:
   a 2% annual management fee (**$3,000,000/yr** just to run the firm) + 20% carried interest above
   an 8% hurdle. Your name is now the brand; LPs bet on you personally. Overlay:
   **$150,000,000 · 2 AND 20**.
7. **The Mega-Platform** — you scale toward the top of the industry. As of January 2026, **Andreessen
   Horowitz and Sequoia Capital each manage roughly $90 billion in AUM** (a16z crossed $90B after a
   record $15B raise — a single fundraise equal to ~18% of all 2025 US VC deployment). 2% of $90B =
   **$1.8 billion a year in management fees alone**, before a dollar of carry is ever realized.
   Overlay: **$90,000,000,000 AUM**.
8. **The Allocators above every fund** — pensions, university endowments (Yale, Harvard), sovereign
   wealth funds (Saudi PIF, Temasek, Mubadala), and funds-of-funds are the LPs who supply the capital
   and decide, fund by fund, whether a GP gets to raise again. **US VC industry AUM: ~$1.25 trillion**
   (NVCA, 2025). **Global VC AUM: ~$3.5 trillion** (2026, up from ~$500B in 2008). They don't pick
   companies. They pick who gets to pick. Overlay: **$3,500,000,000,000**.

## The core mechanic / share-worthy fact: THE POWER LAW

**Correlation Ventures** analyzed ~21,000 VC financings (2004–2013) and found: **~65% of venture
investments return less than the capital put in** (money-losing or break-even); **fewer than 4%
return 10x or more**; **the top 0.4% return 50x+** — and that razor-thin sliver produces the majority
of the industry's total returns. This is the real mechanic behind the title's promise — **"one bet
pays for all."** ✅ (verified, widely corroborated across VC literature — Seth Levine, TechCrunch,
academic citations of the Correlation Ventures dataset.)

## The anti-portfolio (a real, public "wait — that's real?" beat)

Since 1999, **Bessemer Venture Partners** has published its own **"Anti-Portfolio"** — a public,
self-deprecating list of the biggest deals its own partners turned down. Founding partner **David
Cowan passed on meeting Larry Page and Sergey Brin** in a friend's garage in 1999–2000 (Google);
Bessemer also passed on Apple pre-IPO secondary stock ("outrageously expensive" at a $60M valuation),
plus Airbnb, eBay, FedEx, Facebook, PayPal, Tesla, Zoom. ✅ (real, published at bvp.com/anti-portfolio;
verified via multiple secondary sources.) Every VC lives with their own private version of this list.

## Real mechanics / jargon to ground the story

- **Deal flow / sourcing** — the pipeline of pitches; a top firm sees hundreds of decks for every
  check it writes.
- **Term sheet** — the non-binding offer that sets valuation, board seats, and preferences.
- **Cap table / pro-rata rights** — the right (not obligation) to keep your ownership % in the next
  round by writing a follow-on check.
- **Board observer vs. board director** — the difference between watching and a real fiduciary duty.
- **Signaling risk** — if a VC doesn't lead/join a company's NEXT round, the market reads it as a red
  flag about the company, even if the reason is unrelated (fund size, sector focus).
- **Down round / cram-down** — a later round priced below the last one; a portfolio mark gets written
  down, sometimes to near-zero.
- **DPI vs. TVPI** — Distributed to Paid-In (actual cash returned to LPs) vs. Total Value to Paid-In
  (cash + *paper* markups). A fund can show a great TVPI for years while DPI stays at zero — "great on
  paper" is a real, structural VC phenomenon, not a metaphor.
- **J-curve** — a fund's early years show a apparent LOSS (fees paid out, no exits yet) before later
  years turn it around, IF they do.
- **Return the fund** — VC shorthand for a single investment whose eventual value alone would repay
  investors the entire fund; the explicit goal of "the one bet."
- **Dry powder** — committed capital not yet deployed.
- **Vintage year** — the year a fund started investing; funds are judged against peers of the same
  vintage.
- **2 and 20** — 2% annual management fee + 20% carried interest above a preferred return ("hurdle,"
  commonly 8%) — the standard VC/PE fee structure.
- **GP / LP** — General Partner (runs the fund, unlimited liability, makes the calls) / Limited
  Partner (supplies capital, liability limited to their commitment, no say in individual deals).

## Emotional arc (the hardening, VC flavor)

earnest opinion-haver (you fall in love with founders and pitches) → the diligence machine (you learn
to say no, professionally, hundreds of times) → the believer who finally gets to lead a deal and
attaches personally to it → the fiduciary who has to sit on a board and watch a company you championed
struggle → the allocator of other people's retirement money, GP commit of your own on the line → the
brand (your name is the fund now; every LP dinner is an audition) → the platform that moves markets by
existing → the person who has become, functionally, the very "no" that once terrified you at Level 1 —
now you say it nine hundred times a year, to people exactly like you used to be.
