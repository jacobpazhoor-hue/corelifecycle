# Research — POV: You Found a Crypto Startup (Billion or Zero)

Format: **scenario** (a one-off escalating situation, not a job ladder). The viewer IS a first-time
founder who quits a job with $4,200 in savings, launches a token, rides it to a paper nine-figure/
billion-dollar peak, loses most of it to a treasury exploit and a federal investigation, and ends the
episode on the other side of the table — the one who now says "not now" to someone else's deck.
Web-verified July 2026; FLAGGED items are directional/composite. The company, the token, the named
co-founder/rival, and the specific dollar path through each stage are a FICTIONAL COMPOSITE built on
real, sourced mechanics and real historical anchor events (cited below) — no real founder or company
is depicted as fact.

## The spine on screen: MARKET CAP / FDV (big gold number) vs. elapsed DAYS (level label)

Unlike the salary-ladder rank format, there's no promotion here — just a clock and a number that both
only go one direction until they don't. Level label = `LEVEL 0N · DAY XX · [beat]`; the overlay big
number is the token's market cap / fully-diluted valuation (FDV) at that moment, because that's the
number the founder (and the audience) actually watches — not personal net worth, which stays mostly
paper/illiquid the entire episode (an "inverted spine" cousin: the market cap is real and enormous;
what the founder can actually spend is small and lagging, then catastrophically both at once).

## Real mechanics/jargon woven in (sourced; flags noted)

- **Founder/team token allocation & vesting**: team allocations of **~15-20% of total supply** are
  standard; the "gold standard" vesting is **4 years total, 12-month cliff, then a 3-year linear
  vest** — nothing unlocks in year one, ~25% unlocks at the 1-year cliff, the rest drips monthly/
  quarterly after. Any team token-generation-event (TGE) unlock above ~5% is treated by the market as
  a sell-pressure red flag. [VERIFIED — TokenMinds/Redwood Valuation/industry vesting-schedule
  consensus, 2025-2026]
- **Seed round mechanics**: crypto seed rounds commonly raise **$2-5M at $20-30M FDV** via a
  **SAFT (Simple Agreement for Future Tokens)** — investors buy the *right* to tokens at TGE, not
  the tokens themselves, at a discount to the eventual public price; seed investors typically get a
  **6-month cliff / 24-month linear vest** with **0-5% unlocked at TGE**. Seed allocations run
  **5-10% of total supply**; private/strategic rounds **10-15%**. [VERIFIED — Astraea Counsel,
  TokenMinds, Hashed]
- **The brutal base rate**: of **56 VC-backed crypto projects that launched tokens in 2025, only 3**
  (Kaito, Story Protocol, Walrus) **crossed a $1B FDV** — most launched trading *underwater* relative
  to their last private round, leaving early investors (and the founders holding illiquid, vesting
  team tokens) paper-poor even after a "successful" raise. **[VERIFIED, AInvest/Jul 2025 — this is the
  episode's "billion or zero" title mechanic and its share-worthy "wait, that's real?" beat.]**
- **Multisig treasury custody**: most protocol treasuries are secured by a multisig wallet requiring
  **M-of-N signatures** (e.g., 2-of-3, 4-of-9); a treasury with too few required signers, or signers
  whose keys are compromised together, is a single point of failure — this is the real mechanism
  behind the largest DeFi hack ever. [VERIFIED, general multisig/DeFi security practice]
- **The Ronin/Axie Infinity bridge hack (March 2022)**: attackers used **compromised private keys**
  to forge fake withdrawal approvals across two transactions, stealing **173,600 ETH + 25.5M USDC
  (~$625M)** — later attributed by U.S. prosecutors to the North Korea-linked **Lazarus Group**. Sky
  Mavis (Axie's developer) began reimbursing victims that June. [VERIFIED — CoinDesk, TechCrunch,
  DOJ attribution]
- **The Terra/Luna collapse (May 2022)**: at its peak (April 2022) the Terra ecosystem's stablecoin
  UST was the 3rd-largest stablecoin (~$17.5B cap) with LUNA's total market cap **over $40B**. When
  UST began losing its dollar peg on **May 7**, Terraform Labs deployed **$1.5B of its Bitcoin
  reserve** in a failed defense of the peg; by May 9-12 UST was trading under $0.10 and LUNA had
  gone from ~$80 to fractions of a cent — **~$40-45B erased in about three days.** [VERIFIED —
  Bitstamp, MIT Sloan CFI, multiple outlets] Do Kwon, Terraform's founder, was extradited Dec 2024,
  pled guilty Aug 2025, and was **sentenced to 15 years in prison in December 2025** for what the
  judge called "a fraud of epic, generational scale." **[VERIFIED, Bloomberg/CNN/DOJ SDNY — recent
  enough to double as the episode's closing "that's not fiction" gut-punch.]**
- **FTX / Sam Bankman-Fried (Nov 2022 collapse)**: SBF's net worth peaked at **~$26B** (March 2022,
  Forbes/Bloomberg); it fell **94% in a single day (Nov 8, 2022)** to under $1B as FTX's liquidity
  crisis broke, then to functionally zero within days. Prosecutors: FTX customers lost **~$8B**,
  equity investors **~$1.7B**, Alameda lenders **~$1.3B** — commingled customer deposits used to
  plug a hedge-fund hole. SBF was convicted on 7 felony counts and **sentenced to 25 years** (March
  2024); his appeal was denied. [VERIFIED — Bloomberg, CNBC, court reporting]
- **SEC enforcement posture (composite/flagged)**: a **Wells notice** is the SEC's formal warning
  that it intends to recommend charges, giving the recipient a chance to respond before an
  enforcement action is filed — a real, standard SEC procedural step used against numerous crypto
  firms 2021-2025 (Coinbase, Ripple, others). The episode's specific Wells notice, settlement terms,
  and dollar figures are a **FLAGGED fictional composite** built on this real mechanism, not any
  single real case.
- **Post-collapse restructuring pattern**: after Terra's collapse, Terraform Labs actually attempted
  a **fork/relaunch ("Luna 2.0")** distributing new tokens to holders of the old chain — a real,
  documented recovery playbook (however poorly it performed) that the episode's Level 8 "relaunch"
  beat is modeled on. [VERIFIED, widely reported 2022]

## Notes for the writer

- Keep the founder morally grey, not a cartoon villain: the crossed line is the same one Terraform
  actually crossed in real life — spending treasury/reserve funds to defend the price during a panic,
  telling yourself it's temporary. Never depict this as a smart move; the story punishes it.
- The "3 of 56 hit $1B" stat and the Do Kwon sentencing (Dec 2025 — eight months before this episode's
  air date) are both real and recent enough to land as the "wait, that's real?" beat.
- FLAG explicitly in narration/description that the company, token, and every named character are
  fictional composites; the market mechanics, the Ronin hack, the Terra collapse, and the SBF/Do Kwon
  sentences are real and independently documented.
