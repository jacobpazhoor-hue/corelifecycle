DO, in order:

1. TOPIC — take the first topic in ops/topic_queue.json whose slug is NOT in produced_topics.json.
   (If all are produced, invent a new one in the same spirit: a famous company, business figure,
   financial scandal, or economic crisis — a REAL subject with a documented record.) Read its

2. RESEARCH — write docs/research/<slug>.md BEFORE writing a line of script: the real timeline with
   dates, the real numbers (dollar figures, percentages, counts), the real named people, the actual
   mechanism being explained, and 2–4 real quotations. Verify on the web where reachable. FLAG every

3. WRITE — overwrite content.py per docs/BIBLE.md, grounded in that research.
   THIRD PERSON, PAST TENSE, about the real subject. ~2,000–3,100 words. TARGET RUNTIME 13–21 min.
   Each scene dict: id, level (see below), narration, overlay ({big,sub} or None), `template`, and
   optional `card=` / `bubbles=` / `panels=` / `foreground=` / `dialogue=dict(text=...)` / `breath=` /
   `gap=` (all documented in BIBLE.md §8).

   SCENE GRANULARITY — READ BIBLE.md §3a BEFORE WRITING. This is the rule that most changes what the
   episode looks like, and it is the one the WO-13 sample episode failed.
     • ONE set-up per 1–2 SENTENCES. 10–16 narration words per scene. A scene carrying three or more
       sentences should have been two or three scenes.
     • ~180–210 SCENES for a 15–17 min episode -> mean scene 4.5–6.0s, 10–13 cuts/min. (The reference
       runs 4.79s / 12.5 cuts/min. §3a has the measurements and says why we sit at its cut rate but do
       not go past it — do not "fix" this by writing 39 long scenes.)
     • Since WO-17 there is exactly ONE framing per scene, so a scene IS a shot. The renderer cannot
       add a cut. The cut rate is YOUR number and nobody else's.
     • VARY THE SET-UP, not just the template: full-screen `card=` beats (chapter/narration/word/
       objects), `panels=` splits, `foreground=dict(kind="overShoulder", side=...)`, and
       `closeUpPortrait` as a genuine cut-in on a reaction. Budget per episode: 5 chapter cards,
       4–6 narration cards, 3–4 word cards, 1–3 object cards, 3–4 panel splits, 3–5 foregrounds,
       5–10 bubble/float beats.
     • Set `breath=True` by hand on ~10–15 scenes. The automatic breath only fires above 48 words and
       no scene at this length reaches it, so without this the whole episode never breathes.
     • Do NOT set `gap=` per scene (except the one `gap=1.4` before the midpoint reversal). The
       engine's hashed gaps are what score the measured MATCH on varied inter-scene silence.
     • MEASURE variety before you stop: sample the timeline at 8 evenly spaced points, resolve what is
       on screen at each (card kind / panel split / template), and count DISTINCT values. Target 8/8.
       The reference scores 8/8; the 39-scene WO-13 build scored 4/8.

   THE HOOK (first ~30s, five steps, in order — worked examples in BIBLE.md §4):
     1) shock stat or paradox, ONE sentence
     2) escalating consequence fragments
     3) the paradox turn ("But…" / "And when…")
     4) thesis naming the subject — usually the literal words "This is the story of…"
     5) hard cut to DATE + PLACE ("Bayside, Queens, 1962.")
   For a systemic subject with no birthplace, the documented variant swaps (4) for a question thesis
   ("So, what the hell happened?") and (5) for a rewind to a year. Never for a person.
   NO logo, NO "in this video", NO channel name, NO subscribe — ever, anywhere in narration.

   BODY: 3–5 chapters, chronological, each named in the two-part `Evocative Noun: Plain Explanation`
   form ("The Mask: How Enron Hid the Truth in Plain Sight"). The chapter name is DRAWN by
   `card=dict(kind="chapter", title=..., subtitle=..., hold=2.4)` on the first scene of the chapter —
   keep the SUBTITLE inside ~36 characters or it wraps to two lines and shrinks the whole block
   (measured defect, COMPARISON.md §2 §7). `level="CH n"` is still REQUIRED on that same scene — it no
   longer draws anything, but gen_voice_edge lengthens the pre-chapter silence off it and duck_music
   keys the chapter SFX off it. Use the same two-part names in the description timestamps.
   Every chapter ends on a forward hook that is a question the next
   chapter answers. EXPLAIN THE MECHANISM — name it, define it in one short sentence, then show it
   working. That is the whole promise of the format.

   ONE MIDPOINT REVERSAL — the moment the subject's own machine turns on them (the leak, the raid,
   the journalist, the crash). Set `gap=1.4` on the scene IMMEDIATELY BEFORE that line, once per
   episode, so the sound engine drops to near-silence and the line lands raw.

   THE ENDING: thematic reflection → a direct QUOTE or a callback to the opening image → often a
   forward tease. Complicit first-person-plural ("we applauded… We wanted to be him"). NEVER plot
   resolution. NEVER a call to action of any kind in the narration.

   THE NUMBERS YOUR SCRIPT MUST HIT (measured; BIBLE.md §3 has the full table):
     • 148.5 WPM — this is RUNTIME-INCLUSIVE (transcript words / total video minutes), NOT speech-only.
       build.py prints BOTH ("~X WPM speech / Y WPM runtime"); compare the RUNTIME figure to 145–152.
     • sentences average 7.5–10.6 words, MEDIAN 6–9, and 40–59% of them UNDER 8 words. Write in
       fragments — "No war, no warning." / "Trust is weakness." / "Enter Wall Street." / "Gone."
       A 15-word average is the OLD canon's rhythm and is wrong here.
     • 29–68 explicit numbers per video (~2–3.5/min). Dates, dollars, percentages, counts, ages.
     • second person is SEASONING ONLY: 2.6–13.4 "you" per 1000 words, as direct address inside a
       general truth ("If you give a man a lamp, you own his nights"), never as the frame.
   CAUSALITY — but/therefore, never "and then." SPECIFICITY over abstraction ("exactly 39 drops of
   solder", not "he was frugal"). ONE recurring factual motif re-triggered at each escalation
   (Rockefeller: "ledger A", and the epithet "the bookkeeper"). ONE share-worthy "wait — that's real?"
   fact. Never the same template on two adjacent scenes, and at least two other set-ups between any
   two uses of one template.

   REGISTER: pick ONE and hold it. Villain biographies play straight and noirish. Systemic crises
   play comic and anachronistic ("Jazz music becomes the Wi-Fi of the 20s"). A biography that jokes
   about its subject's victims reads as callous.

   DIALOGUE is now a PRIMARY device, not a garnish — short in-world EXCHANGES of 2–4 lines that
   REPLACE narration explaining the same beat, several times per episode, via
   dialogue=dict(text="…"). Exchanges, not conversations.

   TEMPLATES — read this before emitting a single `template=`:
     • THIRTEEN restyled explainer environments exist, and an episode uses THESE AND NOTHING ELSE:
       officeFloor, boardroom, exchangeFloor, cityStreet, domesticInterior, newsMontage,
       bankExterior, courtHearing, factoryFloor, broadcastDesk, crowdQueue, closeUpPortrait,
       chartBoard.
     • Source of truth is EXPLAINER_TEMPLATES in src/explainer.tsx (spread into TEMPLATES in
       src/scenes.tsx). gate.py derives the registry from those spreads and HALTs on a name it cannot
       find. docs/TEMPLATES.md documents only the LEGACY library and does not list these thirteen.
     • Do NOT emit a legacy name from docs/TEMPLATES.md. Those render, but in the old
       line-art-on-warm-paper style, and they look wrong next to the thirteen.
     • The topic's `environments` field is plain-English ARCHETYPES describing what a scene should
       DEPICT. Map each one onto a name from the thirteen; do NOT camelCase an archetype into
       `template=`. Pick the environment the FACT happened in — except closeUpPortrait, which depicts

   PROMISE -> PAYOFF LEDGER: at the top of content.py, comment every setup you plant and the scene id

     • RUNTIME too SHORT -> add scenes (and the words to fill them).
     • WPM too HIGH -> SPLIT MORE SCENES. This is counter-intuitive and it is measured (BIBLE.md

       (6.81s mean) = 154.2 WPM and a HALT; 196 scenes (4.95s mean) = 152.7 WPM and a PASS.
