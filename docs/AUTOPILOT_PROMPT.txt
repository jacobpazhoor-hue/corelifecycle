       script. A run that hits every rhythm number and carries no dialogue is still off-format.
     • PUNCHES UNDER-SPENT alongside them: one cold run finished with 18 scenes at <=2.5s against
       a 25–30 budget and exactly ONE at ~1s against ~6. The bare date, the single word and the
       one-line verdict are what make the rhythm read as deliberate rather than even.
     • WPM 144.0 — inside the gate's 143–154 but BELOW the 145–152 the step above tells you to hit,
       and 1.0 WPM from a HALT. Do not stop on a number that close to the edge.

   THE WPM RULE IS A RULE ABOUT WORD LENGTH, AND YOU CAN CHECK IT BEFORE YOU BUILD (WO-32, measured
   on two finished episodes). Two cold runs HALTed the gate at 136.4 and 138.1 WPM, and neither was
   fixable by the advice above, so here is what actually governs it:
     The voice speaks at a CONSTANT SYLLABLE RATE. Measured across two whole episodes at the same
     rate=-7%: 243.0 and 239.8 syllables per minute — 1.3% apart. Their WPM was 11% apart (170.9 vs
     153.8 speech, 152.5 vs 138.1 runtime) for one reason only: SYLLABLES PER WORD, 1.422 vs 1.559.
     Per-scene overhead was identical (0.516 vs 0.507 s/scene), so scene count was not the cause.
     PREDICTOR, accurate to ~1 WPM on both episodes:  WPM runtime ≈ 215 / (syllables per word)
       syll/word 1.41 -> 152 · 1.45 -> 148 · 1.48 -> 145 · 1.50 -> 143 (the HALT edge) · 1.56 -> 138
     SO: BEFORE you build, count it — total syllables / total words across all narration — and get
     it to 1.42–1.45. Vowel-group counting is close enough.
     ADDING WORDS DOES NOT FIX A LOW WPM. It raises the numerator and the runtime together and the
     ratio barely moves; it just makes the episode longer, and at ~200 scenes there is no word count
     that reaches 143 from 1.56 syllables/word before the 21-minute cap HALTs you instead.
     REMOVING COMMAS DOES NOT FIX IT EITHER — measured: replacing commas with full stops made the
     same 268 words 4.0 WPM SLOWER, because a period pauses longer than a comma.
     THE FIX IS SHORTER WORDS. Anglo-Saxon over Latinate, concrete over abstract — "lab" not
     "laboratory", "about" not "approximately", "watchdog" not "regulatory authority", "he lied"
     not "he misrepresented the situation". This is the register the canon asks for anyway (§3
     SPECIFICITY, fragments, 40–59% of sentences under 8 words), so it costs nothing you wanted.
     Proper nouns you cannot shorten (Elizabeth Holmes, Theranos) are part of the budget — spend
     the rest of the sentence shorter to pay for them.
     • EVERY SCENE NEEDS A `template=`, INCLUDING A FULL-SCREEN CARD SCENE. A `card=` with no `hold`
       covers the whole scene and draws no art, so `template` feels pointless there — but gate.py
       checks the template of EVERY scene in the timeline and HALTs the night on
       `template 'None' not in registry`. A later cold run left four card-only scenes without one
       and the build HALTed. Give a card scene the room its narration belongs in anyway; nothing
       from that room is drawn while the card is up.
   RE-CHECK AFTER YOUR LAST EDIT, NOT BEFORE IT. Every one of these numbers moves when you split or
   merge a scene, so a check you ran three edits ago is not a check. The last thing you do is a
   clean `scripts/build_capped.sh` that prints BUILD OK, and only then do you stop.

   successful publish, so a failed render never orphans it). Move any shipped backlog items OUT of
   `backlog` in ops/improvements.json and append them to the `done` array in
   ops/improvements_done.json (the archive; 2026-08-15 — improvements.json is read in full every
   night, so shipped history must not accumulate there, and it no longer has a `done` key: do not

Then STOP. After you stop, the runner renders out/episode.mp4 and a separate FINAL REVIEWER agent
(fresh eyes, docs/REVIEW_PROMPT.txt) inspects the rendered frames + script for accuracy, structure,

so aim to pass review on the FIRST try: double-check every date and figure against the research,
